This page repeats the read-based rerun without one rule of the tested family, the clustering rule. That rule leaves out any variant with another candidate variant within 30 bases. Everything else is unchanged, and the reads10 page and its results are unchanged.

What changed (our definitions):

  • Dropped: the clustering rule. It was added for the original run, where donors were chosen by their RNA allele fraction: a cluster of variant calls is often misaligned reads rather than real SNPs. Here every tested SNP must already be heterozygous in our genotype panel, so we asked whether the rule still removes anything real.
  • Unchanged: the inclusion labels from each read’s own alignment, panel-heterozygous donors with ≥ 10 reads, every recorded variant × exonic part, ≥ 20 reads over the donors used and a part of ≥ 10 bp in the tested family, the shuffles, isoLASER’s scoring functions, the adaptive rounds, BH within each timepoint, and the cis / |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10 screen.
  • Checks: before comparing, we confirmed for every chromosome × timepoint that both runs are finished and current. They use the same isoLASER recordings, the same association list and genotype inputs, and identical unshuffled scores in round 1, so the runs differ only in the family rule. As in every run, each unshuffled score was rebuilt independently, and all 66 chromosome × timepoint checks matched.
  • Not yet done: calibration runs.

Main result: 45 associations in 22 genes have q < 0.05 and pass the screen, against 42 in 19 genes in the reads10 rerun. No association reported there is lost here. 5 association × timepoint results are gained, in 3 genes; 3 of these genes are not reported in the reads10 rerun at all (ENSG00000289047, FLOT1, HLA-C). All gained genes lie in the MHC, so the read-based results hardly depend on the clustering rule.

A neighbour is a panel variant if our genotype panel has a single matching, well-genotyped record (typed, or imputed with R² ≥ 0.8), whatever the donors’ genotypes. It is an unsupported call if the panel has no such record, or if it is not a single-base variant. Most newly tested variants sit next to real panel variants, that is, two real SNPs close together.

All 5 gained results come from three genes in the MHC, and the clustering rule had removed every one of them:

  • HLA-C (0 and 6 hours) and FLOT1 (1 hour): the only neighbour is an unsupported call, the pattern the rule was designed to catch.
  • ENSG00000289047 (1 and 6 hours): the neighbour is a panel variant that is heterozygous in a donor, so this one looks more plausible, although it is in the MHC too.

So with read-based labels the clustering rule removes 11,689 testable associations (summed over timepoints), but almost no signal outside the MHC (our reading).

How the test works

The test is the one described on the original page, with the donor rule and remaining family rules of the reads10 rerun.

Stopped means at least 20 hits at that checkpoint, or no observed score (p = 1). Every association with zero hits after a million shuffles has q < 0.05, so more shuffles would not change the results.
