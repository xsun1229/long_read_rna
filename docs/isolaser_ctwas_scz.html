We ran cTWAS on the schizophrenia (SCZ) GWAS with our isoLASER associations as splicing QTLs. cTWAS asks two things: whether the splicing QTLs explain more GWAS heritability than SNPs in general (enrichment), and which genes’ splicing is likely causal (PIP, the posterior inclusion probability). We ran it for each timepoint separately, and with all timepoints pooled.

Short answer.

  • Enrichment: the splicing QTLs are enriched in every run. The log_enrichment for separate timepoints is 4.2–5.5 read-based and 3.3–5.3 transcript-based; pooled, it is 4.74 and 3.99.
  • prop_heritability of the splicing group: small, 0.0029–0.0177, because even the largest run has only 1,386 traits against millions of SNPs.
  • Genes: 11 genes reach a gene-level combined_pip of 0.8 in at least one run: ACTB, CNPPD1, GOPC, HSPA9, LY6H, MYT1L, NDRG4, NLRP1, NRP1, PIGF, SF3B1. LY6H, our GWAS Catalog highlight for smoking, reaches 0.8 at every read-based timepoint.
  • Pooling gives 819 read-based and 1,386 transcript-based traits, and 5 and 3 genes at combined_pip 0.8. Genes not at 0.8 in any single-timepoint run: read-based none; transcript-based none.
  • Joint run (read-based, the three timepoints as separate groups in one run): one combined_pip per gene over all its parts and timepoints. 7 genes reach 0.8 (LY6H, NDRG4, MYT1L, SF3B1, HSPA9, CNPPD1, PIGF) and 18 reach 0.5. The separate read-based runs had 8 genes at 0.8 between them; the joint run adds none.

How the runs were set up

  • GWAS: PGC schizophrenia, ieu-b-5102 (N = 127,906), z-scores harmonized to the UK Biobank (UKB) LD reference, from the earlier multigroup cTWAS project.
  • Molecular traits (our definition): one trait per tested exonic part. A part is included when one of its associations has isoLASER’s cis label, computed from the panel-heterozygous donors only, with no ΔPSI or FDR cutoff. This is the first set of the GTEx comparison.
  • QTL and weight: each trait has one SNP, the part’s cis variant with the smallest permutation p (ties: largest |ΔPSI|), among single-base variants in the UKB reference that are not strand-ambiguous. The weight is ΔPSI (ALT minus REF inclusion) with ALT as the effect allele. With one SNP per trait, only the weight’s sign matters.
  • Mapping table: each trait (exonic part) maps to the gene the part belongs to. It is written with the weights and used for gene-level PIPs.
  • Three kinds of runs: one run per timepoint; one run per version with all timepoints pooled into a single group (how); and one read-based run with the three timepoints as separate groups (how).
  • Settings: UKB b38 LD, no thinning, one prior variance shared by all groups (shared_all), up to 5 signals per region (L = 5), at least 100 traits per group. Regions are fine-mapped when their non-SNP PIP is at least 0.5.
  • Not done yet: merging regions and checking LD mismatch.

What the tables and figures show

Parameters, the output of summarize_param():

  • group_size: splicing traits that reach cTWAS after matching to the GWAS and the LD reference.
  • group_prior: the estimated prior probability that a trait (splicing) or a variant (SNP) is causal.
  • group_prior_var: the prior variance of a causal effect, shared by both groups (shared_all).
  • log_enrichment: the log of the splicing prior over the SNP prior, with its standard error; enrichment_pval is its p-value.
  • group_pve: the proportion of SCZ variance explained by the group: group_size × group_prior × group_prior_var / GWAS N. total_pve sums the splicing and SNP groups.
  • prop_heritability: a group’s group_pve over total_pve, the slices of the pie charts.

Convergence, from make_convergence_plots(): the estimates at each iteration of the parameter estimation. Proportion causal (π) is group_prior, effect size (σ2) is group_prior_var, enrichment (πG/πS, not on the log scale), and PVE. Flat lines at the end mean the estimates have settled.

Gene-level PIPs: combined_pip combines a gene’s exonic parts with combine_gene_pips() (method = "combine_cs", filter_cs = FALSE: all parts, whether or not they are in a credible set). It sums the parts’ susie_pip within each credible set and combines credible sets as 1 − ∏(1 − sum). Parts that share a top SNP are identical predictors and split their PIP, so a gene’s combined_pip can be high when none of its parts is. The gene tables list genes with combined_pip of at least 0.5:

  • Parts: the gene’s parts in fine-mapped regions.
  • Top part: the part with the largest susie_pip, its susie_pip, and its z-score (z).
  • QTL: the top part’s SNP. ΔPSI and permutation p are its isoLASER association, from our permutation test. In pooled runs, QTL from gives the timepoint the QTL was taken from.

All timepoints jointly

How the joint run works (Xin’s suggestion; read-based only): one run with the three timepoints as separate groups, 0hr|splicing, 1hr|splicing and 6hr|splicing, each with that timepoint’s weights (the traits of the separate runs) and its own group_prior; group_prior_var is shared. A part tested at several timepoints enters once per timepoint. Copies with the same QTL are identical predictors and split their PIP, but they fall in the same credible set, so a gene’s combined_pip (all its parts and timepoints, combine_gene_pips() with by = "type") adds them back.
