This page compares read-based isoLASER without the clustering filter (FDR page) with GTEx v8 sQTLs in the nine brain tissues that have full summary statistics. As in the right panel of Fig. 3e of the isoLASER paper, we compare our allelic splicing effects (isoLASER ΔPSI) with GTEx sQTL effects (beta), using each event’s skipping junction. Its usage falls when the exonic part is included, so a negative correlation is expected.

How pairs were matched

  • Variant and gene: the same SNV (position, REF and ALT; isoLASER’s 0-based position + 1 = GTEx’s), tested for the same Ensembl gene in GTEx.
  • GTEx data: the full summary statistics, every tested association at any p-value. Nine of GTEx’s 13 brain tissues have them on the cluster. The other four (Amygdala, Anterior cingulate cortex BA24, Spinal cord cervical c-1 and Substantia nigra) have only GTEx’s significant pairs and are left out. This differs from the paper, whose Methods describe an overlap with GTEx’s significant pairs: here pairs that GTEx does not call significant (the grey points) are included.
  • Event: the exonic part must form an annotated exon-skipping or alternative-splice-site event, and the GTEx junction must be exactly that event’s skipping junction.
  • Points: each point is one pair: an isoLASER association (variant × exonic part) and its matched GTEx skipping junction, at one timepoint.

Our associations come in three sets:

  • Paper’s cutoff: isoLASER’s own cis label and pooled ΔPSI, as in the paper, from the read-based run’s joint output. It does not depend on our filters.
  • Before FDR: this run’s screen. The donors are panel-heterozygous with ≥ 10 reads, with ≥ 20 reads over those donors, cis, |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05 and part ≥ 10 bp. There is no clustering filter.
  • After FDR: the same plus permutation BH q < 0.05 within each timepoint, as on this run’s FDR page.

For this GTEx comparison, we use a broader effect-size screen of |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05; the main FDR page uses ≥ 0.10. The BH q-values were calculated over the full eligible family of tested associations in each timepoint, before the cis and |ΔPSI| display filters are applied, so the broader screen changes no q-value. It only shows more of the associations that already have q < 0.05.

Example, HNRNPK. The C>T variant (chr9:83980154, 0-based) and the 34-base exonic part (gold) form an alternative splice site. GTEx measures the skipping junction (blue), which jumps over the part. At 6 hours, ALT changes part inclusion by -26.5 percentage points in this run. GTEx Cerebellum’s beta for the skipping junction is +0.97: opposite signs, as expected.

Testing

How the testing works

Two tests, both on the skipping-junction pairs, for each set and tissue:

  • Correlation (as in the paper): the Pearson correlation of our ΔPSI with GTEx’s beta, for each timepoint. A negative R is expected. Expected sign counts the pairs where the two have opposite signs. GTEx p < 1e-5 repeats the count for pairs where GTEx itself has strong evidence.
  • Sign consistency (our definition; the paper reports only the correlation):
    • we take the pairs where GTEx is significant (nominal p < 0.05, the blue points) and our ΔPSI is not 0;
    • a pair is consistent when our ΔPSI and GTEx’s beta have opposite signs;
    • a binomial test asks whether the share of consistent pairs differs from 50% (two-sided) or is above it, the expected direction (one-sided).
    We count this per timepoint, and over all timepoints with each pair once: each pair’s mean ΔPSI over the timepoints where it is called, so an association reported at several timepoints counts once.

The binomial p-values are exploratory. The test assumes independent pairs, and they are not:

  • pairs can come from the same gene or exonic part, so related variants count separately;
  • the same associations recur in every tissue;
  • nothing corrects for the many sets, timepoints and tissues tested.

For example, after FDR the pairs with GTEx p < 0.05 are 5 pairs at 4 exonic parts in 4 genes (ATP5MJ, HNRNPK, PPA2, SNHG5), over all nine tissues. Their agreement is useful descriptive evidence, but the binomial p treats them as 5 independent trials. A formal test would have to account for dependence within genes and loci, and for multiple comparisons.

Gene-level overlap with GTEx sGenes

One tab per tissue, with the same layout as the scatter plots: rows are the three sets and columns the timepoints. The numbers in each panel are:

  • isoLASER genes only: genes with at least one association of the set at that timepoint that are not GTEx sGenes;
  • both: genes that are both;
  • GTEx sGenes only: GTEx sGenes of the tissue (gene-level q < 0.05) without an association of ours.

Each panel is restricted to genes tested in both studies at that timepoint: in our permutation run and by GTEx in that tissue. This overlap is at the gene level. A gene counts as shared when GTEx finds an sQTL in it through any variant and junction, not necessarily our SNV or our event. The scatter plots require both, so their gene counts are smaller.
