This page compares read-based isoLASER without the clustering filter (FDR page) with GTEx v8 Cerebellum sQTLs. The comparison with the filter remains as an earlier setup.

As before, we compare our allelic splicing effects (isoLASER ΔPSI) with GTEx v8 Cerebellum sQTL effects (beta), as in the right panel of Fig. 3e of the isoLASER paper. We use each event’s skipping junction, whose usage falls when the exonic part is included, so a negative correlation is expected.

How pairs were matched

The matching is unchanged:

  • Variant and gene: the same SNV (position, REF and ALT; isoLASER’s 0-based position + 1 = GTEx’s) tested for the same Ensembl gene in GTEx.
  • GTEx data: full Cerebellum summary statistics, every tested association at any p-value.
  • Event: the exonic part must form an annotated exon-skipping or alternative-splice-site event, and the GTEx junction must be exactly that event’s skipping junction.
  • Points: each point is one pair: an isoLASER association (variant × exonic part) and its matched GTEx skipping junction, at one timepoint.

Our associations come in three sets:

  • Paper’s cutoff: isoLASER’s own cis label and pooled ΔPSI, as in the paper, from the read-based run’s joint output. It does not depend on our family rules, so it is the same set as on the page with the clustering filter.
  • Before FDR: this run’s screen. The donors are panel-heterozygous with ≥ 10 reads, with ≥ 20 reads over those donors, cis, |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10 and part ≥ 10 bp; variants with another candidate variant within 30 bp are kept.
  • After FDR: this run’s screen plus permutation BH q < 0.05 within each timepoint, as on this run’s FDR page.

Example, HNRNPK. The C>T variant (chr9:83980154, 0-based) and the 34-base exonic part (gold) form an alternative splice site. GTEx measures the skipping junction (blue), which jumps over the part. At 6 hours, ALT changes part inclusion by -26.5 percentage points in this run. GTEx’s beta for the skipping junction is +0.97: opposite signs, as expected.

Direction where GTEx is significant

As on the original page, we take pairs where GTEx is significant (nominal p < 0.05, the blue points). A pair is consistent when our ΔPSI and GTEx’s beta have opposite signs. A binomial test asks whether the share of consistent pairs differs from 50%. These definitions are our own; the paper reports only the correlation.

Dropping the clustering filter changes little here. Before FDR it adds a few pairs, and the correlation stays negative at every timepoint (similar or slightly stronger than with the filter); after FDR the pairs are the same, because the gained associations, all in the MHC, have no matched GTEx pair. The paper’s-cutoff rows are the same set in both runs.
