Rmd a43035e XSun 2026-10-28 update

This page compares transcript-based isoLASER without the clustering filter (FDR page) with GTEx v8 sQTLs in the nine brain tissues that have full summary statistics. As in the right panel of Fig. 3e of the isoLASER paper, we compare our allelic splicing effects (isoLASER ΔPSI) with GTEx sQTL effects (beta), using each event’s skipping junction. Its usage falls when the exonic part is included, so a negative correlation is expected.

How pairs were matched

  • Variant and gene: the same SNV (position, REF and ALT; isoLASER’s 0-based position + 1 = GTEx’s), tested for the same Ensembl gene in GTEx.
  • GTEx data: the full summary statistics, every tested association at any p-value. Nine of GTEx’s 13 brain tissues have them on the cluster. The other four (Amygdala, Anterior cingulate cortex BA24, Spinal cord cervical c-1 and Substantia nigra) have only GTEx’s significant pairs and are left out. This differs from the paper, whose Methods describe an overlap with GTEx’s significant pairs: here pairs that GTEx does not call significant (the grey points) are included.
  • Event: the exonic part must form an annotated exon-skipping or alternative-splice-site event, and the GTEx junction must be exactly that event’s skipping junction.
  • Points: each point is one pair: an isoLASER association (variant × exonic part) and its matched GTEx skipping junction, at one timepoint.

Our associations come in three sets:

  • isoLASER cis, heterozygous donors (our definition): isoLASER’s own cis cutoff, computed in this run from the panel-heterozygous donors only (≥ 10 reads each, ≥ 20 over those donors, part ≥ 10 bp), with no ΔPSI or q cutoff. It replaces the paper’s cutoff of the earlier version, isoLASER’s joint output. That set also used variants absent from the donors’ genotype panel, and pooled homozygous donors.
  • Before FDR: the same plus |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05: this run’s screen. There is no clustering filter.
  • After FDR: the same plus permutation BH q < 0.10 within each timepoint, as on this run’s FDR page.

The cutoffs are those of this run’s FDR page: |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05 and BH q < 0.10. The BH q-values were calculated over the full eligible family of tested associations in each timepoint, before the cis and |ΔPSI| display filters are applied, so the effect-size screen changes no q-value. Earlier versions used q < 0.05 (page) and the paper’s cutoff as the first set (page).

Example, HNRNPK. The C>T variant (chr9:83980154, 0-based) and the 34-base exonic part (gold) form an alternative splice site. GTEx measures the skipping junction (blue), which jumps over the part. At 6 hours, ALT changes part inclusion by -21.4 percentage points in this run. GTEx Cerebellum’s beta for the skipping junction is +0.97: opposite signs, as expected.

Testing

How the testing works

Two tests, both on the skipping-junction pairs, for each set and tissue:

  • Correlation (as in the paper): the Pearson correlation of our ΔPSI with GTEx’s beta, for each timepoint. A negative R is expected. Expected sign counts the pairs where the two have opposite signs. GTEx p < 1e-5 repeats the count for pairs where GTEx itself has strong evidence.
  • Sign consistency (our definition; the paper reports only the correlation):
    • we take the pairs where GTEx is significant (nominal p < 0.05, the blue points) and our ΔPSI is not 0;
    • a pair is consistent when our ΔPSI and GTEx’s beta have opposite signs;
    • a binomial test asks whether the share of consistent pairs differs from 50% (two-sided) or is above it, the expected direction (one-sided).
    We count this per timepoint, and over all timepoints with each pair once: each pair’s mean ΔPSI over the timepoints where it is called, so an association reported at several timepoints counts once.

The binomial p-values are exploratory. The test assumes independent pairs, and they are not:

  • pairs can come from the same gene or exonic part, so related variants count separately;
  • the same associations recur in every tissue;
  • nothing corrects for the many sets, timepoints and tissues tested.

For example, after FDR the pairs with GTEx p < 0.05 are 17 pairs at 8 exonic parts in 8 genes (ARF4, ATP5MJ, DNTTIP2, HNRNPK, PFKP, SNHG5, TAX1BP1, ZFAND2A), over all nine tissues, and the binomial p treats them as 17 independent trials. A formal test would have to account for dependence within genes and loci, and for multiple comparisons.

Estimated non-null proportion among GTEx-matched associations (Storey’s π1)

For each association in the cis and before-FDR sets, we take the GTEx p-value of its event’s skipping junction for our SNV, as in the scatter plots, and estimate Storey’s π1 from these p-values, for each timepoint separately. When GTEx tests several skipping junctions of the event (2% of matched associations), we use the one shared by the most annotated transcripts that skip the part, chosen without looking at GTEx p-values. This is exploratory.

What it estimates: GTEx p-values of associations with no effect in GTEx are spread evenly between 0 and 1, and real effects tend to produce smaller p-values when GTEx has sufficient power. The estimator measures the flat part, π0, from the larger p-values, and π1 = 1 − π0 is the estimated proportion of non-null associations among matched GTEx tests. Limited power can make weak non-null effects look null and underestimate π1, so π1 cannot distinguish false positives in our study from context-specific effects or effects that GTEx has insufficient power to detect. It also ignores direction, which is checked under Testing.

After FDR: only 9–22 associations are matched per tissue and timepoint, too few for a stable π1: one p-value crossing 0.5 moves it by 2/n, 0.22 at n = 9. Each tab lists them instead, with their GTEx p-value and direction. Expected direction means GTEx’s beta has the opposite sign to our ΔPSI at every timepoint where the association is reported, as in the sign-consistency test.
