We ran cTWAS on the schizophrenia (SCZ) GWAS with our isoLASER associations as splicing QTLs, using isoLASER + filter: isoLASER’s cis label plus |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05. This page repeats the joint run of the earlier cTWAS page, which used the cis label alone, with these weights. cTWAS asks whether the splicing QTLs explain more GWAS heritability than SNPs in general (enrichment), and which genes’ splicing is likely causal (PIP, the posterior inclusion probability).

Short answer.

  • Enrichment: log_enrichment is 4.64 at 0 hr and 5.81 at 6 hr. At 1 hr the prior goes to about 0: in the joint run the 1 hr traits explain nothing beyond the 0 and 6 hr traits (why).
  • prop_heritability of the splicing groups: 1.67% together, most of it at 6 hr.
  • Genes: 9 genes reach combined_pip 0.8: LY6H, NDRG4, MYT1L, SF3B1, CNPPD1, PIGF, GOPC, MRPL43, YWHAE. With the cis label alone there were 7. The filter removes HSPA9 and adds GOPC, MRPL43, YWHAE.
  • The filter removes the high-coverage cis calls: above about 2,370 reads isoLASER’s cis cutoff falls below 0, so nearly every association there is labelled cis with ΔPSI near 0. On the earlier page ACTB, COX6C and RPL13 rested on such parts; none of them is in a fine-mapped region now.

How the runs were set up

  • GWAS: PGC schizophrenia, ieu-b-5102 (N = 127,906), z-scores harmonized to the UK Biobank (UKB) LD reference, from the earlier multigroup cTWAS project.
  • Molecular traits (our definition): one trait per tested exonic part. A part is included when one of its associations has isoLASER’s cis label, computed from the panel-heterozygous donors with at least 10 reads each, and |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05 (isoLASER + filter; no FDR cutoff). That is 194 / 359 / 403 associations at 0 / 1 / 6 hr.
  • QTL and weight: each trait has one SNP, the part’s variant with the smallest permutation p (ties: largest |ΔPSI|) among its associations that pass the filter, single-base variants in the UKB reference that are not strand-ambiguous. The weight is ΔPSI (ALT minus REF inclusion) with ALT as the effect allele. With one SNP per trait, only the weight’s sign matters. This gives 153 / 242 / 302 traits, of which cTWAS keeps those whose SNP is in the GWAS (group_size below).
  • Mapping table: each trait (exonic part) maps to the gene the part belongs to. It is written with the weights and used for gene-level PIPs.
  • One run: the three timepoints as separate groups in one run (how), with the same settings as the earlier page.
  • Settings: UKB b38 LD, no thinning, one prior variance shared by all groups (shared_all), up to 5 signals per region (L = 5), at least 100 traits per group. Regions are fine-mapped when their non-SNP PIP is at least 0.5.
  • Not done yet: merging regions and checking LD mismatch.

What the tables and figures show

Parameters, the output of summarize_param():

  • group_size: splicing traits that reach cTWAS after matching to the GWAS and the LD reference.
  • group_prior: the estimated prior probability that a trait (splicing) or a variant (SNP) is causal.
  • group_prior_var: the prior variance of a causal effect, shared by all groups (shared_all).
  • log_enrichment: the log of a splicing group’s prior over the SNP prior, with its standard error; enrichment_pval is its p-value.
  • group_pve: the proportion of SCZ variance explained by the group: group_size × group_prior × group_prior_var / GWAS N. total_pve sums all groups.
  • prop_heritability: a group’s group_pve over total_pve, the slices of the pie chart.

Convergence, from make_convergence_plots(): the estimates at each iteration of the parameter estimation. Proportion causal (π) is group_prior, effect size (σ2) is group_prior_var, enrichment (πG/πS, not on the log scale), and PVE. Flat lines at the end mean the estimates have settled.

Gene-level PIPs: combined_pip combines a gene’s exonic parts with combine_gene_pips() (method = "combine_cs", filter_cs = FALSE: all parts, whether or not they are in a credible set). It sums the parts’ susie_pip within each credible set and combines credible sets as 1 − ∏(1 − sum). Parts that share a top SNP are identical predictors and split their PIP, so a gene’s combined_pip can be high when none of its parts is.

All timepoints jointly

How the joint run works (Xin’s suggestion; read-based only): one run with the three timepoints as separate groups, 0hr|splicing, 1hr|splicing and 6hr|splicing, each with that timepoint’s weights and its own group_prior; group_prior_var is shared. A part tested at several timepoints enters once per timepoint. Copies with the same QTL are identical predictors and split their PIP, but they fall in the same credible set, so a gene’s combined_pip (all its parts and timepoints, combine_gene_pips() with by = "type") adds them back.

1 hr (ours): its group_prior is 6e-08, so its log_enrichment is very negative and its standard error meaningless. In a joint run the timepoints compete for the same GWAS signal, and the 1 hr traits explain nothing beyond the 0 and 6 hr traits. Identical traits alone don’t explain it: about 20% of 1 hr traits are copies of a 0 or 6 hr trait, as with the cis label alone, where 1 hr kept a log_enrichment of 3.53.

Plausibility of the genes at combined_pip 0.8

NDRG4, SF3B1 and MYT1L sit at genome-wide significant SCZ loci, and LY6H has the strongest splicing effect. YWHAE has a sub-threshold signal here but a PGC3 SCZ association 3 kb from its SNP. GOPC, MRPL43, CNPPD1 and PIGF are at loci without a genome-wide significant SNP within 250 kb; their PIP rests on the high 6 hr enrichment.   • PIP and part counts: include all fine-mapped predictors for the gene. A dash means no fine-mapped predictor at that timepoint. The calculation uses combine_gene_pips(method="combine_cs", filter_cs=FALSE), with by="context" for each timepoint and by="type" for the overall splicing PIP.
  • Splicing effects: summarize parts with individual predictor PIP at least 0.05, grouped by timepoint and QTL SNP. With the filter, no part has 2,371 or more reads.
  • Delta PSI: ALT inclusion minus REF inclusion; +0.250 means +25 percentage points. Permutation p is unadjusted. Reads are pooled informative reads from the heterozygous donors. Ranges summarize several parts, not confidence intervals; low counts can produce uncertain effect estimates.
  • Selected SNP: the QTL of the part with the largest predictor PIP. The GWAS columns refer to that SNP and its surrounding 250 kb on either side in the SCZ GWAS used here (ieu-b-5102). Genome-wide significance is p < 5e-8.
  • GWAS Catalog (new genes only): the nearest association whose mapped trait contains “schizophrenia” with p ≤ 5e-8, within 500 kb (GRCh38). It can come from a larger GWAS than the one used here.
  • GTEx support: a significant SNP-intron association for the same SNP and gene in GTEx v8 brain tissues. It need not measure the same exonic part, so it is not automatically replication of the specific splicing event. No match in these significant-pair files does not establish absence of an effect.
  • Interpretation: high cTWAS PIP supports a predictor under the fitted model; it does not prove that the particular splicing event mediates SCZ risk. With one-SNP predictors, separation from the SNP depends strongly on the model’s priors. The high 6 hr enrichment can favor predictors even at suggestive loci.
