We asked whether the variants behind our allele-specific splicing associations are known GWAS hits for neuropsychiatric traits. For each variant we read its entries in the GWAS Catalog, the same list the Catalog shows on its page for the rsID. A shared hit means the variant affects both splicing and the trait in different studies. It does not show that the splicing change causes the trait.

This page uses the current cutoffs, BH q < 0.10 and |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05. The earlier page used q < 0.05. The lower q adds 7 read-based genes (ASRGL1, DGCR6L, EIF6, NME2, PATJ, SDHA, TMEM9) and 7 transcript-based genes (CNIH1, CUTA, LRPPRC, NDUFA2, NME2, PATJ, XPA). None of them carries a brain-related hit, so the hits below are the same as on the earlier page.

Short answer. 4 of the 30 genes reported by read-based isoLASER carry a variant that is itself a genome-wide GWAS hit for a neuropsychiatric, brain or behaviour trait. LY6H is the one to highlight:

  • Our variant, rs9694368, is the GWAS Catalog lead SNP for smoking initiation (p = 3 × 10-24), and the Catalog maps that signal to LY6H.
  • LY6H limits the function of α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in neurons, the receptors nicotine acts on.
  • The association is about the use of LY6H-201’s first exon. It has the same direction at 0, 1 and 6 hours (+17, +15 and +23 pp) and is significant at 6 hours (q = 0.0014).
  • It was also a follow-up gene in the earlier read-based run with the clustering filter, with identical numbers. Both runs use the same reads, so this shows the result does not depend on the filter; it is not an independent replication.
  • With transcript-based labels the effect is +1 to +6 pp (q = 1), so only the read-based labels find it.

The other three genes are weaker:

  • GNL3: intelligence. Our association also looks like the same splicing event as a published GNL3 splicing QTL in brain.
  • HNRNPK: a sleep health score.
  • FLOT1: major depression. It lies in the MHC, so this one is weak.

None of the transcript-based variants is a brain-related hit.

How the lookup works

  1. Start from our reported associations. We use the current runs, without the clustering filter. An association is one variant × exonic part at one timepoint. It is reported when q < 0.10, isoLASER labels it cis and |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.05. Read-based isoLASER reports 87 associations, at 56 exonic parts in 30 genes (transcript-based: 177, 80 and 41). The earlier cutoff, |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10, is shown for comparison.
  2. Keep one variant per exonic part. A part often has several reported variants on the same haplotype. For each gene × exonic part, we keep the reported variant nearest to the part (distance 0 when inside it). This leaves 32 of 38 read-based variants.
  3. Find each variant’s rsID. We take it from the record in our genotype panel with the same position, REF and ALT. Every variant has one.
  4. Look up the variant in the GWAS Catalog.
    • We use the Catalog release of 2026-10-22: 1,203,840 entries for 452,283 SNPs.
    • We match entries by the rsID, or by the same GRCh38 position, and both always agree here.
    • This gives the same list as the Catalog’s page for the rsID. For example, rs1108842 (GNL3) has four entries: intelligence and three for adiponectin levels.
    • 8 of the 32 read-based variants have at least one entry.
  5. Keep genome-wide hits (p ≤ 5 × 10-8). The Catalog also accepts entries with p down to 10-5, but every entry matched here is genome-wide.
  6. Keep neuropsychiatric and brain traits. Each trait is classed as a neuropsychiatric disorder or a brain or behaviour trait. The rules and the traits found are under Trait classes.
  7. Summarise per gene. For each gene and class, we show the entry with the smallest p.

Trait classes

The grouping is ours. Each Catalog entry maps to one or more traits (EFO terms), and each trait gets a class:

  • Neuropsychiatric disorder: the Catalog’s parent category is “Neurological disorder” (the Catalog files psychiatric disorders there too). Or the trait name contains one of these: psychiatric, schizo, bipolar, depress, autism, attention deficit, anxiety, panic, obsessive, personality disorder, anorexia, bulimia, eating disorder, tourette, substance, addict, dependence, alcohol use disorder, suicid, psychos, mental.
  • Brain or behaviour: the trait name contains one of these: intellig, cognit, educational attainment, years of schooling, mathemat, reading, memory, executive, reaction time, neurotic, mood, worry, loneliness, well-being, happiness, feeling, risk-taking, risk tolerance, behavio, personality, insomnia, sleep, chronotype, smoking, cigarette, alcohol, drinking, cannabis, caffeine, brain, cortical, cortex, cerebral, hippocamp, amygdala, thalam, putamen, caudate, pallidum, accumbens, white matter, grey matter, gray matter, subcortical, intracranial, neurite, fractional anisotropy, antidepressant, antipsychotic, lithium, headache, migraine. A trait that also contains protein, level of, in blood, serum, plasma, cerebrospinal is excluded, so blood levels of brain proteins do not count.

Traits in neither class are not shown: blood counts, body size, metabolic traits and so on. An entry whose traits fall in both classes counts as a neuropsychiatric disorder.

Interaction and adjusted analyses also map their exposure as a trait. In “waist-hip ratio × smoking” or “… adjusted for smoking”, for example, smoking is mapped next to the main trait. Those exposure traits are ignored unless the main trait is itself one of them. The exposures are smoking, cigarette, tobacco, alcohol, drinking, physical activity, sleep duration, educational attainment.
