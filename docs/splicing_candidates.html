Which genes change how their exons are joined between 0, 1 and 6 hours after stimulation? This page covers how candidates were selected, what came out, what those genes do, and four worked examples.

This is a screen for picking events worth looking at, not a differential splicing analysis: no replicate-aware model, no multiple-testing correction, and library depth is confounded with timepoint.

1. How candidates were selected

Counting junctions

Junctions are read directly from the alignments — every N operation in a CIGAR string is one intron, recorded per cell. Only primary alignments (-F 0x900) from donor-assigned cells on chr1–chr22 are counted, so counts are comparable across timepoints and can be checked per donor.

Scoring usage, not abundance

A gene that is simply induced raises all of its junctions together. To avoid ranking induction as splicing, each junction is scored by its share of reads at the same splice site, not by raw count:

usage = reads on this junction / reads on all junctions at that site

computed separately within every donor × timepoint. Junctions are grouped both by shared genomic start and by shared genomic end, so competitors are compared whichever side they have in common.

Why the depth rule is asymmetric

An earlier version required 20 reads at the site in every donor at both timepoints. That rule discards induced genes by construction: a gene that is off at baseline and switched on by stimulation has low 0 hr depth by definition. It removed ATF3, BDNF, FOS, HOMER1, NR4A1, SIK1 and TIPARP — 7 of the 12 detectable canonical immediate-early genes — along with 1,410 genes in total.

Requiring 20 reads only at the deeper timepoint, and 10 at the shallower one, keeps those genes while still demanding enough reads to say a junction is genuinely absent rather than merely unobserved. Junctions with zero reads now score usage 0 instead of being dropped, which is what makes an on/off switch like BDNF visible at all.

The change is purely additive: every gene found by the old screen is still here, plus 292 more.

2. The candidate genes

1,022 junctions across 392 genes. Sorted by the largest absolute usage change per gene. events uses strand to name the splice site correctly — a shared genomic start is the intron’s donor on the plus strand and its acceptor on the minus strand.

Full tables: candidate_genes_v2.tsv (gene level) and candidate_junctions_v2.tsv (junction level, with coordinates, per-donor ΔPSI range, and usage at each timepoint).

Most genes contribute two rows to the junction file with equal and opposite ΔPSI. That is by construction — the two competing junctions at one shared site — not two independent findings.

Each candidate is labelled by function: whether it is a known stimulation-response gene, a known neuronal gene, or neither.

Labelling rules, so the categories can be checked:

  • activity-regulated — the gene is on a curated list of immediate-early and secondary activity-response genes (FOS/JUN/EGR/NR4A families, ARC, NPAS4, BDNF, ATF3, CREM, HOMER1, VGF, SCG2, HDAC9, …).
  • neuronal — four or more GO terms matching neuron|synap|axon|dendri|neurotransmit|neurite|nerve|neural|glia.
  • neuronal (weak annotation) — one to three such terms. Often a single incidental term on a gene that is not really neuronal, so read these individually.

4. Gene examples

All figures use the same reads: donor-assigned cells, primary alignments only, native depth. Arc labels are supporting-read counts and were checked against the junction tables.

Reading a panel: filled areas are read depth, curves are splice junctions, and the number on a curve is how many reads support that junction. Per-donor CREM junction usage at the shared acceptor.

The exon at 35,195,127–35,195,214 is a transcript start for ENST00000356917 and three other models, roughly 68 kb inside a gene that begins at 35,126,791. So the switch is from the gene’s own 5′ end to an internal alternative promoter, and it is close to complete: 0.47 → 0.04 for the full-length start, 0.15 → 0.76 for the internal one.

That architecture — an internal, activity-inducible promoter within CREM — is how ICER is produced. We have not confirmed that these reads correspond to the annotated ICER first exon specifically; what is verified here is an internal promoter, strongly induced.

BDNF — an on/off switch the earlier screen could not see

BDNF’s 5′ non-coding exons each have their own promoter and all splice to one common coding exon, so junctions at the shared acceptor chr11:27,658,586 report promoter usage.

A true zero at baseline in all four donors, and the tightest cross-donor agreement at 6 hr anywhere in this dataset. Exon IV is the canonical CREB/NPAS4-responsive BDNF promoter (hg38 promoter chr11:27,701,325–27,702,402); exon I is the other classic activity-induced promoter. Both fire.

Locus reads are 1,236 → 6,890 → 9,696, about 4.6× induction after normalising to genome-wide junction depth.

Site depth at 0 hr is 21/10/15/13 reads across the four donors. Under the old rule — 20 reads in every donor at both timepoints — three of four donors failed and BDNF was dropped before any ΔPSI was computed.

Like CREM, this is a promoter switch rather than a splicing change. Resolving which promoter an individual molecule used is something long reads do well and short reads do not.

Monotonic in all four donors, mean ΔPSI +0.457. Splicing efficiency is flat across timepoints, so this is site choice rather than a change in how completely the intron is removed.

CLDND1 is not a known activity-response gene, and its highest CNS expression is in corpus callosum, so it is likely glia-enriched. A shift in the neuron/glia proportion of nuclei across timepoints would produce this figure without any within-cell splicing change, and the donor-consistency filter gives no protection — all four donors share the same composition drift.

NRXN2 — a neuronal candidate, read with caution

chr11, minus strand. Three junctions at the terminal intron; the novel cryptic one declines while the annotated one rises.

NRXN2 terminal intron across 0, 1 and 6 hours.

junction 0 hr 1 hr 6 hr status
J1 64,608,083–64,620,293 62 94 112 annotated last intron
J2 64,607,501–64,620,293 62 93 81 annotated, alternative acceptor
J3 64,608,083–64,608,563 49 49 37 novel cryptic donor

Per-donor usage of all three NRXN2 junctions.

Three reasons for caution:

  • Depth is thin — 29–67 reads per donor per timepoint for the whole locus, so J3 rests on 6–14 reads per donor. CD_36’s 0 hr value of 0.48 comes from 29 reads and does much of the visual work.
  • Genome-wide the share of reads on non-GENCODE junctions also falls (14.81% → 13.08% → 13.79%, consistent in all four donors). NRXN2’s 12-point drop is ~8× that drift, so it survives — but the drift is real.
  • This is not SS4. Neurexin splice site 4 is the textbook activity-dependent event, but it lies in the LNS6 domain, far 5′ of this terminal intron. The gene name alone does not carry that precedent.

The neurexin literature has already been caught by the composition confounder raised for CLDND1: depolarising cortical cultures produced an apparent Nrxn1-SS4 shift by killing neurons but not astroglia.

Still outstanding

  1. A permutation null. Shuffle timepoint labels within donor and rerun, to estimate how many of the 1,022 are noise. Random 4/4 sign agreement alone occurs 12.5% of the time and there is no multiple-testing correction.
  2. A cell-type split. Every candidate here is vulnerable to a shift in the neuron/glia composition of nuclei across timepoints. CLDND1 is the clearest case where this must be settled before the result can be called splicing.
  3. Promoter versus splicing. The two strongest results, CREM and BDNF, are alternative-promoter events. They belong in the results but should be labelled as such rather than counted as splicing changes.
