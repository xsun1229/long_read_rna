Two examples

We screened splice junctions in the 0, 1 and 6 hour libraries for changes that occur in the same direction in all four donors. The two examples show different kinds of transcript change: CREM has a strong internal-promoter signal, while TTLL7 shows a change consistent with skipping an annotated exon. CREM has a documented activity-response role; TTLL7 has an experimentally supported neuronal role, but its changing junction has not been functionally validated in this stimulation experiment.

CREM: an internal-promoter-associated junction

CREM has one clear changing junction: a junction from an internal exon to the common downstream exon rises from 9/60 (15.0%) of reads at that acceptor at baseline to 270/414 (65.2%) at 1 hour and 941/1,241 (75.8%) at 6 hours.

CREM is an activity-responsive gene. An alternative intronic promoter is known to produce the inducible CREM isoform ICER (Molina et al., 1993). The observed junction starts beside an annotated internal CREM transcript start, so alternative promoter usage is a plausible explanation. We have not established that these particular molecules are ICER transcripts. The figure shows a change in junction usage; it does not by itself prove within-cell regulation by stimulation.

CREM usage calculation

At this locus, several junctions end at the same acceptor, chr10:35,206,894. For each timepoint we add the counts of all junctions ending there, across the four donor-assigned cell sets. The usage rate is:

CREM internal-junction usage = reads on the 35,195,215–35,206,894 junction ÷ reads on every junction ending at 35,206,894.

Time after stimulation Target-junction reads All junction reads at the acceptor Target usage
0 hr 9 60 15.0%
1 hr 270 414 65.2%
6 hr 941 1,241 75.8%

For example, at 6 hr: 941 ÷ 1,241 = 0.758, or 75.8%. The denominator includes rare alternatives not labeled on the sashimi plot. These are fractions of junction-supporting reads, not measured fractions of full-length CREM transcripts. Using fractions matters because total read depth differs greatly among timepoints.

We checked each donor separately before pooling. The same junction gains usage from 0 to 6 hr in all four donors:

Donor 0 hr 6 hr
CD_21 5/23 (21.7%) 274/371 (73.9%)
CD_31 0/12 (0%) 305/388 (78.6%)
CD_36 0/10 (0%) 141/199 (70.9%)
CD_48 4/15 (26.7%) 221/283 (78.1%)

The pooled percentages above give greater weight to donors with more reads. The candidate screen instead averages the four donor-specific changes, giving each donor equal weight; for this junction its mean change from 0 to 6 hr is +0.633, or 63.3 percentage points.

TTLL7: an exon-skipping pattern in a neuronal gene

TTLL7 modifies tubulin in neurons. Experimental depletion suppressed nerve-growth-factor-stimulated neurite growth in PC12 cells and reduced beta-tubulin polyglutamylation in primary neurons (Ikegami et al., 2006). That supports a neuronal role for the gene, but does not establish that this particular junction is regulated by stimulation or changes TTLL7 protein function.

TTLL7 sashimi plot at 0, 1 and 6 hours, pooling primary alignments from four donor-assigned cell sets at each timepoint. The short junction has 573, 1153 and 1066 reads; the competing long junction has 81, 336 and 1116 reads.

TTLL7, chr1, minus strand. Numbers on curves are supporting reads, not percentages. The short arc is at the far left; the long arc reaches from the left to the far right. Arc placement above or below the coverage has no biological meaning. The black track is one GENCODE reference transcript.

Both arcs end at the same TTLL7 exon boundary at chr1:83,951,977. The short junction spans 83,951,977–83,952,186, entering the intervening annotated exon. The long junction spans 83,951,977–83,998,930 and bypasses that 201-nucleotide exon. In the minus-strand transcript, this is consistent with skipping the second exon of the displayed reference model. We have not established complete isoforms or the protein consequence.

TTLL7 long-junction usage = reads on the 83,951,977–83,998,930 junction ÷ reads on every junction starting at 83,951,977.

Time after stimulation Short-junction reads Long-junction reads All junction reads at shared site Long-junction usage
0 hr 573 81 659 12.3%
1 hr 1,153 336 1,491 22.5%
6 hr 1,066 1,116 2,198 50.8%

At 6 hr, 1,116 ÷ 2,198 = 50.8%. The denominator includes 16 reads on other rare junctions at this boundary, which is why it exceeds 1,066 + 1,116. The long-junction usage rises in each of the four donors: from 7.3–16.0% at baseline to 46.8–57.1% at 6 hr. The mean of the four donor-specific changes is +39.8 percentage points. As for CREM, these are fractions of junction-supporting reads, not fractions of full-length transcripts; changes in cell composition could also affect the pattern.

How candidates were selected

The screen counts every N operation in the BAM alignment CIGAR as a junction. It uses primary alignments from donor-assigned cells on chromosomes 1–22, counted separately by donor and timepoint. Junctions are compared when they share either genomic boundary; usage is calculated within that shared-site group. An unobserved junction is assigned zero usage only when that site has enough reads to evaluate it.

A candidate needs at least 10 reads at the site per donor at the shallower timepoint and 20 at the deeper timepoint, a consistent direction of change in all four donors, and an average change of at least 15 percentage points. The screen compares 0→1, 0→6 and 1→6 hr. Under these exploratory rules it yielded 1,022 junction rows across 392 genes. Rows may describe competing junctions at the same site, so they are not 1,022 independent splicing events.

CREM and TTLL7 were chosen because their junction shifts are large and consistent across all four donors, and because their genes have documented activity-responsive and neuronal roles, respectively. The two examples illustrate different transcript structures. The displayed counts were checked against donor-specific junction tables and the filtered BAM inputs. The screen has no formal replicate-aware test or multiple-testing correction, and the current analysis pools cell types within each donor.

The complete candidate gene table is available for exploring other loci. BDNF is another strong activity-responsive example, but its main change concerns alternative first exons/promoters. The observed NRXN2 junction is a terminal-intron event and should not be labeled as the classic neurexin SS4 event.

The relevant scripts are 7.sashimi/1.extract_junctions.sbatch and 7.sashimi/14.find_candidates_v2.R. Counts and candidate tables are in long_read_processed/junctions/. The plotted regional BAMs and the CREM_sashimi.png source figure is in 7.sashimi/out/; the TTLL7 source figure and donor-level usage table are in 7.sashimi/out/ttll7_review/.
