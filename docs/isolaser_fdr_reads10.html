Earlier setup. This page keeps the clustering filter, which the current analyses drop. Current pages: read-based isoLASER (main analysis) and transcript-based isoLASER.

This page repeats transcript-based isoLASER with one change: each read’s inclusion label comes from its own alignment. The transcript-based page and its results are unchanged.

What changed (our definitions):

  • How isoLASER labels reads: isoLASER decides whether a read includes or excludes an exonic part from the transcript the read was assigned to, not from the read’s own alignment. A read assigned to a transcript that retains an intron counts as retaining it, even if the read splices it out. For example, isoLASER counted 28 ALT reads as keeping the tested KPNA2 intron of the transcript-based page; in their alignments, 20 of them splice it out (our read check).
  • Read-based labels: a modified copy of isoLASER labels each read from its alignment. A read includes the part if at least 10 of its aligned bases fall inside it (all of them, for parts shorter than 10 bases), and excludes it if one of its splice junctions spans the whole part. Other reads (those that don’t reach the part) get no label and are not counted. Variant calls, phasing and allele assignments are unchanged: every read keeps the allele it had in the original run.
  • Unchanged from the transcript-based run: panel-heterozygous donors with ≥ 10 reads, every recorded variant × exonic part, the tested family (≥ 20 reads over the donors used, part ≥ 10 bp, and no other candidate variant within 30 bp), the shuffles, isoLASER’s scoring functions, the adaptive rounds, BH within each timepoint, and the cis / |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10 screen.
  • Checks: the modified run’s allele assignments match the original run’s exactly, on all 66 chromosome × timepoint combinations. The unshuffled score of every tested association was rebuilt independently, from isoLASER’s own summary tables, and matched.
  • Not yet done: calibration runs.

Main result: 42 associations in 19 genes have q < 0.05 and pass the screen, against 64 in 26 genes in the transcript-based run. 7 genes are reported in both. The reported effects of the transcript-based run mostly vanish with read-based labels, while this run’s effects are already visible, in the same direction, in the transcript-based run (details).

Stopped means at least 20 hits at that checkpoint, or no observed score (p = 1). Every association with zero hits after a million shuffles has q < 0.05, so more shuffles would not change the results.

How to read each gene’s figures:

  • Permutation test: one panel per timepoint. Grey bars are the first 1,000 shuffled scores; the blue line is the observed score (AMI). The p-value uses the full adaptive run, up to a million shuffles.
  • Read alignments: isoLASER’s nigiri_plot sashimi renderer with the project’s plotting fixes. Reads are split into REF (blue) and ALT (orange) and pooled over the donors used at each timepoint. Yellow marks the tested part. Coverage and arc numbers are raw read counts, on one scale per gene. Raw inclusion is each read’s own label, so every counted read reaches the part; these are the counts the test uses before it normalizes donors.
  • Isoforms and haplotypes, at 0 hours and at the significant timepoint, drawn as on the transcript-based page:
    • Rows: the transcripts the reads were assigned to, grouped by whether the transcript’s structure includes the part. With read-based labels, a read’s own label can differ from its transcript’s group.
    • Numbers: reads per transcript from each haplotype, raw counts summed over the donors used.
    • PSI row: each haplotype’s inclusion from the reads’ own labels, the raw version of the test’s table. A note under the row gives any reads assigned to transcripts that don’t span the part: they count in PSI but are not drawn.
    • Test line: the test’s ΔPSI and q.

PARK7

The tested 46-base part is the extra stretch of PARK7-201’s longer first exon. Its donor site lies 58 bases beyond the other transcripts’ donor site, an alternative 5′ splice site; the transcript-based page tested the adjacent 12-base part of the same stretch. One minor transcript (ENST00000465354) has this part as its whole first exon, which is why the figure’s automatic label reads “alternative first exon”.

ALT (C) reads use the longer first exon less often than REF (T) reads. The effect has the same direction at all three timepoints and in both donors (CD_21 and CD_48), and is largest at 1 hour: −19 pp (q = 0.002), with REF 74% vs ALT 51% and REF 73% vs ALT 57% in the two donors. At 0 and 6 hours it is −8 and −10 pp. The variant (rs226249) lies in the first exon, 18 bases before the shorter exon’s donor site.

PPA2

PPA2 encodes the mitochondrial inorganic pyrophosphatase. DDG2P lists a PPA2-related sudden cardiac death disorder, which is not a brain disorder. The tested 27-base part is the last stretch of an 87-base cassette exon. PPA2-202 skips the whole exon, and the reads that exclude the part skip the whole exon: none use the exon’s shorter form.

ALT (G) reads include the exon more often than REF (C) reads. The effect is largest at 1 hour: +46 pp (q = 0.004), from REF 33% vs ALT 77% raw inclusion, with +53 and +36 pp in the two donors (CD_31 and CD_48). At 0 and 6 hours it is +21 and +18 pp, below threshold; CD_36, with 6 to 16 reads per allele, varies between −5 and +12 pp. The tested SNP (rs13787) is in a downstream exon, about 42 kb away in the genome. GTEx Cerebellum has a strong sQTL at the same SNP for this exon’s skipping junction (p = 1 × 10⁻¹⁹), with the opposite sign to our ΔPSI, as expected (GTEx page).

Isoforms and haplotypes

LY6H

The tested 12-base part is the start of LY6H-201’s first exon. LY6H-202 and LY6H-205 start at an exon further upstream and splice past the part into an exon inside LY6H-201’s first exon. Read coverage ends at LY6H-201’s start site, so reads that include the part start there, and reads that exclude it splice from the upstream exon. The test therefore compares the use of two first exons (our reading of the alignments and annotation).

ALT (G) reads include the part more often than REF (A) reads, so they use the upstream first exon less. The effect has the same direction at all three timepoints and in both donors (CD_21 and CD_31), and is largest at 6 hours: +25 pp (q = 0.001), from REF 66% vs ALT 92% raw inclusion, with +30 and +20 pp in the two donors. At 0 and 1 hours it is +15 and +14 pp, below threshold. The tested SNP (rs10109061) is in the last exon, 1.9 kb from the part. LY6H belongs to the Ly6 family of GPI-anchored, neurotoxin-like proteins. Published work (not our data) reports that it limits surface α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in neurons.

ZNF749

As on the transcript-based page, the tested part is a 355-base alternative last exon. REF (G) reads end in it far more often than ALT (C) reads. The difference is significant only at 6 hours (−74 pp, q = 0.001); at 0 and 1 hours it is −26 and −8 pp. In our stimulation splicing scan, the balance between the two donor sites that splice into this exon shifts between 0 and 6 hours.

As before, the jump at 6 hours partly reflects which donors are used:

  • CD_21 has a large effect at every timepoint: −72, −43 and −81 pp.
  • CD_48 shows no difference at 0 hours (+1 pp, from 8 ALT reads) and a large one at 6 hours (−66 pp).
  • CD_36 has an inconsistent effect (−19 pp at 0 hours, +18 pp at 1 hour) and no reads at 6 hours.
