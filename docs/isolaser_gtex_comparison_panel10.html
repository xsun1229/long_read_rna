This page repeats the comparison with GTEx Cerebellum sQTLs for the genotype-panel rerun of our permutation test. In that rerun, a donor is used only if our genotype panel calls it heterozygous, and associations come from every recorded variant × exonic part. The original page and its results are unchanged.

As before, we compare our allelic splicing effects (isoLASER ΔPSI) with GTEx v8 Cerebellum sQTL effects (beta), as in the right panel of Fig. 3e of the isoLASER paper. We use each event’s skipping junction, whose usage falls when the exonic part is included, so a negative correlation is expected.

How pairs were matched

The matching is unchanged:

  • Variant and gene: the same SNV (position, REF and ALT; isoLASER’s 0-based position + 1 = GTEx’s) tested for the same Ensembl gene in GTEx.
  • GTEx data: full Cerebellum summary statistics, every tested association at any p-value.
  • Event: the exonic part must form an annotated exon-skipping or alternative-splice-site event, and the GTEx junction must be exactly that event’s skipping junction.
  • Points: each point is one pair: an isoLASER association (variant × exonic part) and its matched GTEx skipping junction, at one timepoint.

Our associations come in three sets:

  • Paper’s cutoff: isoLASER’s own cis label and pooled ΔPSI, as in the paper. This set doesn’t depend on our donor rule, so it is the same as on the original page.
  • Before FDR: the rerun’s screen. The donors are panel-heterozygous with ≥ 10 reads, with ≥ 20 reads over those donors, cis, |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10, part ≥ 10 bp, and no other candidate variant within 30 bp.
  • After FDR: the rerun’s screen plus permutation BH q < 0.05 within each timepoint, as on the rerun’s FDR page.

Example, HNRNPK. The C>T variant (chr9:83980154, 0-based) and the 34-base exonic part (gold) form an alternative splice site. GTEx measures the skipping junction (blue), which jumps over the part. At 6 hours, ALT changes part inclusion by -21.4 percentage points in the rerun. GTEx’s beta for the skipping junction is +0.97: opposite signs, as expected.

Direction where GTEx is significant

As on the original page, we take pairs where GTEx is significant (nominal p < 0.05, the blue points). A pair is consistent when our ΔPSI and GTEx’s beta have opposite signs. A binomial test asks whether the share of consistent pairs differs from 50%. The paper’s-cutoff rows repeat the original page’s table, since that set doesn’t depend on our donor rule. These definitions are our own; the paper reports only the correlation.
