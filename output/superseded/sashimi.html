Question and result

Can we see a change in how reads connect exons after neuronal stimulation? Here we focus on CLDND1, comparing 0 hr (baseline), 1 hr, and 6 hr in batch 21, with cells assigned to CD_21, CD_31, CD_36, and CD_48.

CLDND1 shows a progressive shift from proximal to distal splice-donor usage. Among reads supporting the two main competing junctions, the shorter junction’s share falls from approximately 74% to 28%, while the longer junction’s share rises from 26% to 72%. All four donors show the same direction of change.

These are junction-supporting read fractions, not estimates of full-length transcript proportions. This is an exploratory example of a time-associated splicing change; it is not a formal differential-splicing test or proof of a response within each neuronal cell type.

Reading the sashimi plot

Figure 1. CLDND1 on chromosome 3, minus strand. Inputs contain primary alignments from donor-assigned cells. All available reads in the selected region are used, without the subsampling used in the earlier equal-depth figure. The coverage tracks use a common scale and represent raw read depth.

Element Meaning
Blue, orange, green panels 0 hr, 1 hr, 6 hr, respectively
Horizontal axis Genomic position on chromosome 3 (GRCh38)
Filled colored areas Number of reads covering each genomic position
Curved line A splice junction: reads align on either side of an intron, skipping its sequence
Number on a curve Number of reads supporting that particular junction
Black rectangles at the bottom Annotated exons in the displayed transcript models
One annotation row One reference transcript; this is not an individual read

The placement of an arc above or below the coverage is a layout choice. It does not indicate increased/decreased usage or the strand of the gene. A long read can cross more than one junction and contribute to several arc counts, so adding every number in a panel does not give the number of reads.

Start with two curves

Find the short curve labeled 141 at baseline. It connects the left exon to the nearby exon boundary. Now find the longer curve labeled 50 that starts at that same left boundary and extends farther to the right.

These are the two competing junctions discussed here. Follow them through the panels:

Junction 0 hr 1 hr 6 hr
Shorter / proximal 141 272 185
Longer / distal 50 241 483
Total for these two junctions 191 513 668

For example, 141 means 141 reads support removal of the intron represented by that short arc. It does not mean 141 reads skip an exon. The other labels, such as 97 and 25 in the baseline panel, describe other junctions with different endpoints and are not part of this two-choice calculation.

Why counts alone are not enough

The shorter junction increases from 141 to 185 reads between baseline and 6 hr, but that does not mean its relative usage increases. There are also many more reads supporting its competitor.

For this simple two-junction comparison:

Shorter-junction share = shorter count / (shorter count + longer count).

Longer-junction share = longer count / (shorter count + longer count).

Share among the two main junctions 0 hr 1 hr 6 hr
Shorter / proximal 73.8% 53.0% 27.7%
Longer / distal 26.2% 47.0% 72.3%

At baseline, the shorter connection predominates. At 1 hr the two are close to equally used. At 6 hr, the longer connection predominates.

The evidence for a change is the shift in relative junction usage, not the increase in the height of a coverage track. Raw coverage also depends on library depth, cell numbers, and gene expression.

Which splice sites are changing?

CLDND1 is on the minus strand, so its transcriptional direction is right to left, opposite to the increasing genomic coordinates. The shared left boundary is the acceptor; the two competing right boundaries are donors. This is a local alternative 5′ splice-site (alternative donor) pattern.

Connection Intron interval, 1-based inclusive Length
Shorter / proximal donor chr3:98,521,443–98,521,652 210 bp
Longer / distal donor chr3:98,521,443–98,522,848 1,406 bp

The coordinates above describe the intronic bases counted by the junction extraction script. The adjacent exon boundaries are 98,521,442 on the left and 98,521,653 or 98,522,849 on the right.

This local junction change does not establish the full splice chain or the abundance of any one annotated transcript. Other junctions in the same region show that the locus has more structure than these two choices alone.

All four donors show increasing distal usage. This supports a consistent pattern rather than a pooled shift driven only by different donor weights. Cell-type composition remains a possible contributor because the current counts pool neuronal cell types within each donor.

Why these percentages differ slightly from the simple calculation

The two-junction table above ignores rare competing junctions to make the arithmetic easy to follow. The screening code and donor plot include those rare alternatives in their denominator.

Pooled across donors 0 hr 1 hr 6 hr
Reads supporting other junctions at the shared site 2 3 5
All junction reads at this site 193 516 673
Distal share using all alternatives 25.9% 46.7% 71.8%

Also, a pooled read fraction weights donors by their coverage. The candidate screen averages the four donor-specific changes equally. Its mean distal change is +0.457, or 45.7 percentage points, from 0 to 6 hr.

How the example was selected and checked

The candidate screen uses primary alignments from donor-assigned cells on chr1–chr22. It counts introns from N operations in the alignment CIGAR, then compares junction usage within each shared left genomic boundary.

For a candidate to be retained in a comparison, the current script requires:

  • At least 20 junction reads at the shared site for each donor and timepoint.
  • The junction to be observed in all four donors at both timepoints.
  • The same direction of change in all four donors.
  • An absolute mean donor-level usage change of at least 0.15.

CLDND1 was a leading candidate for visual inspection. Its regional BAMs were independently recounted, and the donor-specific counts at the shared site matched the saved junction tables at all three timepoints.

This screen has no replicate-aware statistical model or multiple-testing correction. It currently excludes zero-count junctions rather than assigning zero usage when the site is adequately covered, and it only groups shared left boundaries. These limitations matter for discovering other events; they do not change the verified CLDND1 counts shown here.
