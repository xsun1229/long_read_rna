We ran isoLASER jointly across four donors on all 22 autosomes, at each of the three timepoints (0, 1, and 6 hours after stimulation). isoLASER phases reads onto haplotypes using heterozygous variants it discovers in the RNA, then tests whether the two copies of each gene splice differently — allele-specific splicing, reported as dPSI (the difference in Percent Spliced In between haplotypes). The dPSI reported here is from the joint model across donors; per-donor allele-specific rates have not been examined separately.

The model used is --platform Nanopore, which loads the default GM12878_ONT_ROC classifier. A second ONT classifier (GTEx_ONT_F1) ships with isoLASER but is currently commented out upstream; we tested both and found F1 recovers more heterozygous sites, but use the default here pending clarification from the developers.

Filtering

Four filters were applied to the raw isoLASER cis calls:

  1. Effect size: |dPSI| >= 0.10. Without this, many statistically significant calls have dPSI of 0.01–0.02, driven by high read depth rather than a meaningful splicing difference.

  2. Clustered variants excluded (conservative heuristic). When multiple variants cluster within 30 bp, they may reflect sequencing errors in difficult sequence context. This is a conservative exclusion — some real variants in complex regions will also be removed. 34% of all variants fall in such clusters.

  3. Per-donor allelic balance. At least one donor must have allelic balance 0.25–0.75 and read depth >= 10 at the phasing variant. This is checked per donor, not averaged across donors — an average can hide a case where no individual donor is heterozygous (e.g., one reference-only and one alternate-only donor averaging to 0.5). This confirms phasing eligibility in at least one donor but does not verify within-donor allele-specific splicing.

  4. Minimum exonic part length >= 10 bp. The exon model splits overlapping annotations into non-overlapping parts, which can create fragments of 1–3 bp. Splicing measured over such short regions is unreliable.

Interpretation

This analysis identifies 1156 genes with at least one event passing quality filters. Each gene is represented by a fixed (variant, exonic part) combination tracked across all three timepoints.

329 genes had usable data at baseline (a heterozygous donor present) with no passing signal, and then passed filters after stimulation. These are differences in signal detection between timepoints. Whether the underlying allele-specific splicing effect itself changes with stimulation — as opposed to changes in power, expression level, or isoform composition — would require comparing reference-versus-alternate splicing rates within individual heterozygous donors at each timepoint.

Another 469 genes had no usable baseline data for the selected event, so baseline absence cannot be assessed.

Caveats:

  • Joint model only. The dPSI values are from isoLASER’s joint analysis across donors. Within-donor allele-specific splicing rates have not been examined.
  • Concordance, not sensitivity. The variant validation page measures agreement between isoLASER calls and the genotype panel; it does not estimate what fraction of true variants isoLASER detects, because it starts from isoLASER’s calls.
  • Four donors. With n = 4, a signal requires a heterozygous variant in at least one donor. Rare variants are not testable.
  • No multiple-testing correction has been applied. The p-values from isoLASER’s joint model are shown as-is.

Related pages: variant validation, junction screen, isoLASER setup.
