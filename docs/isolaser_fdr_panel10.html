This page repeats the genome-wide FDR analysis with a different way of choosing donors and associations. Many variant calls behind the original results have no support in the donors’ own genotype panel (see technical artifacts in isoLASER variant calls). The original page and its results are unchanged.

What changed (our definitions):

  • Donors: a donor is used for an association only if our genotype panel calls it heterozygous at that variant. The panel site must be directly genotyped or imputed with R² ≥ 0.8, with the same two alleles. The donor also needs ≥ 10 informative reads, isoLASER’s own default. The original rule was ≥ 30 reads and an RNA alternative-allele fraction of 0.25–0.75.
  • Associations: every variant × exonic part recorded in any donor. The original run used only the rows of isoLASER’s joint output, which drops parts seen in a single donor.
  • Tested family: additionally ≥ 20 raw reads summed over the donors used. Fewer can never give the ≥ 10 reads per allele that isoLASER needs to score an association.
  • Unchanged: the shuffles, the scoring with isoLASER’s functions, the adaptive rounds, BH within each timepoint, and the cis / |ΔPSI| ≥ 0.10 screen applied afterwards.
  • Not yet done: calibration runs. The original calibration used a different donor rule, so it does not carry over to this run.

Main result: 64 associations in 26 genes have q < 0.05 and pass the screen, against 375 in 131 genes originally. 7 genes are significant at all three timepoints.

How the test works

The test is the one described on the original page. Only the donor and family rules above differ. An association’s eligible donors are fixed before shuffling, from read counts and the panel genotype, neither of which a shuffle changes. Eligible associations without a computable observed score stay in the family with p = 1.

Timepoint Stopped at 1,000 Stopped at 10,000 Stopped at 100,000 Reached 1,000,000
0hr 39,753 295 68 48
1hr 60,186 509 91 80
6hr 62,597 497 63 66

Stopped means at least 20 hits at that checkpoint, or no observed score (p = 1). Every association with zero hits after a million shuffles has q < 0.05, so more shuffles would not change the results.

Real data only; there are no calibration runs for this donor rule. The bulk lies below the diagonal: many associations tie with their shuffles at an AMI of 0, which isoLASER rounds to three decimals, so their p-values are conservative. The upper plateau is the minimum attainable p-value.

How to read each gene’s figures:

  • Permutation test: one panel per timepoint. Grey bars are the first 1,000 shuffled scores; the blue line is the observed score (AMI). The p-value uses the full adaptive run, up to a million shuffles.
  • Read alignments: isoLASER’s nigiri_plot sashimi renderer with the project’s plotting fixes. Reads are split into REF (blue) and ALT (orange) by isoLASER’s allele assignments and pooled over the donors used at each timepoint (heterozygous in the panel, ≥ 10 reads). Yellow marks the tested part. Coverage and arc numbers are raw read counts, on one scale per gene. Raw inclusion comes from isoLASER’s transcript assignment, so a read can count without reaching the plotted region. Each plot’s subtitles give how many reads reach it. The test uses donor-normalized tables, so its dPSI can differ slightly from the raw counts.
  • Isoforms and haplotypes, at 0 hours and at the significant timepoint, drawn like Fig. 2d of the isoLASER paper:
    • Rows: annotated transcripts with their GENCODE v44 name and Ensembl ID. Novel transcripts are IsoQuant models from our annotation, shown with their SQANTI3 category against GENCODE v44. NIC is a new combination of known splice sites; fusion spans two genes.
    • Which rows: the three transcripts with the most reads in each group are drawn, and a grey row sums the rest. Introns are shortened.
    • Marks: green is the tested part. Orange marks the tested SNP, plus the nearest in-phase SNP when that one is closer to the part.
    • Numbers: reads per transcript from each haplotype, labelled by the tested SNP’s alleles, as raw counts summed over the donors used.
    • PSI row: each haplotype’s inclusion from those raw counts: reads in transcripts that include the part, divided by all its reads, drawn and undrawn transcripts together.
    • Test line: ΔPSI and q are the test’s values. The test pools donor-normalized tables, so its ΔPSI can differ slightly from the difference between the two raw PSIs.

KPNA2

The tested part is a whole 100-base intron between exons 6 and 7. KPNA2-221 keeps it, and the other annotated transcripts splice it out. isoLASER counts ALT (C) reads as keeping the intron more often than REF (T) reads: at 6 hours, 12.7% vs 2.5% (+12 pp, q = 0.016). At 0 and 1 hours the difference is +8 and +2 pp, below threshold. The tested SNP (rs12752) is in the first exon, 7.2 kb upstream. In the three donors, all 20 heterozygous panel SNPs within the gene are phased the same way, so T and C reads come from two whole-gene haplotypes. The nearest SNP to the intron, rs4638, is 135 bases downstream, with G on the T haplotype.

PARK7

The first exon ends at one of two donor sites 58 bases apart, an alternative 5′ splice site, and both sites splice to the same next exon. The tested 12-base part is the extra stretch of the longer first exon; the adjacent 46-base part gives the same result. ALT (C) reads use the shorter exon more often than REF (T) reads. The difference has the same direction at all three timepoints and in both donors, and is largest at 1 hour (−17 pp, q = 0.002), the only significant timepoint; at 0 and 6 hours it is −7 and −8 pp. This describes each timepoint separately and is not a test of a change over time. The variant (rs226249) lies in the first exon, 18 bases before the shorter exon’s donor site.

ZNF749

The tested part is a 355-base alternative last exon. Transcripts that use it end there, and the others continue to exons further downstream. REF (G) reads end in it far more often than ALT (C) reads. The difference is significant only at 6 hours (−74 pp, q = 0.002); at 0 and 1 hours it is −26 and −17 pp and below threshold. In our stimulation splicing scan, the balance between the two donor sites that splice into this exon shifts between 0 and 6 hours.

The jump at 6 hours partly reflects which donors are used:

  • CD_21 has a large effect at every timepoint: REF 92% vs ALT 15% inclusion at 0 hours, 86% vs 6% at 6 hours.
  • CD_48 shows no difference at 0 hours, from only 8 ALT reads, and a large one at 6 hours (85% vs 19%).
  • CD_36 has an inconsistent effect (−19 pp at 0 hours, +8 pp at 1 hour) and no reads at 6 hours.
